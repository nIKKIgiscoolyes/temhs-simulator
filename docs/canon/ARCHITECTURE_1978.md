# 13. LEVELS -1 THROUGH -30 — 1978 TEMHS

The giant underground complex originates primarily from:

# 1978

The playable -1 through -4 levels must preserve unmistakable late-1970s institutional architecture.

Use:

- poured concrete;
- exposed structural concrete;
- rough aggregate;
- heavy cylindrical columns;
- rectangular concrete columns;
- waffle slabs;
- structural ceiling grids;
- enormous beams;
- terrazzo;
- older VCT flooring;
- concrete walls;
- old institutional doors;
- metal lockers;
- visible conduit;
- surface-mounted electrical systems;
- exposed piping;
- ventilation grilles;
- fluorescent fixture housings;
- cork boards;
- blackboards;
- old cabinetry;
- old stair towers;
- deep door openings;
- strange transitions;
- low-ceiling service connectors;
- curved tunnels;
- utility spaces;
- extensive mechanical systems.

The original underground complex included large amounts of:

- utilities;
- storage;
- circulation;
- service infrastructure.

Some original service networks contain:

- narrow rounded tunnels;
- lower clearance;
- turns;
- elevation changes;
- limited natural visibility.

These characteristics belong primarily in service/restricted infrastructure.

Do not make normal occupied academic corridors claustrophobic merely to emphasize age.

# 14. UNDERGROUND TEMHS IS NOT ABANDONED

This requirement is absolute.

Do NOT make underground TEMHS:

- horror;
- abandoned;
- post-apocalyptic;
- ruined;
- infected;
- SCP-like;
- dungeon-like;
- supernatural;
- mold-covered;
- dangerously dark;
- full of flickering horror lights.

It is an active school.

The correct aesthetic is:

> A gigantic 1978 institutional complex that has been continuously used, repaired, patched, cleaned, rewired, retrofitted, modified, remediated, and partially modernized for almost fifty years.

Old and new infrastructure must coexist.

Example room:

- 1978 concrete structure;
- original terrazzo;
- old chalkboard;
- 1990s cabinets;
- newer projector;
- modern Wi-Fi;
- modern fire alarm;
- new LED-retrofitted fixture;
- old clock;
- 2025 environmental sensor;
- 2026 TMAP repair;
- contemporary student desks.

Layered history is essential.

Do not make every renovated area look brand-new.

Do not make every old area look unsafe.

# 18. TMAP VISUAL LANGUAGE

Especially underground, occasionally show:

- sealed repairs;
- environmental monitors;
- HEPA equipment;
- new conduit beside old conduit;
- newer fire doors in old openings;
- patched floors;
- renovated rooms beside less-renovated rooms;
- updated electrical panels;
- temporary barriers;
- inspection stickers;
- contractor carts;
- recently painted surfaces;
- older surfaces awaiting work;
- ceiling access;
- temporary directional signage;
- work notices;
- modern ventilation equipment;
- repaired concrete.

Active work zones are separated from ordinary students.

Students should not casually walk through contractor work areas.
